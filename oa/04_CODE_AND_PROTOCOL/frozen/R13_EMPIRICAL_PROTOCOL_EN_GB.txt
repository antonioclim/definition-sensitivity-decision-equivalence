ODSA R13: EMPIRICAL AMENDMENT FOR FAT AND OPEN ACCESS
Protocol identifier: ODSA-R13-FAT-OA-DESCRIPTIVE-20261111
Prepared on 11 November 2026
Status: specified for the final R13 freeze; the new empirical analyses have not been executed

1. Purpose, authority and limits

This amendment specifies two bounded descriptive applications of the ODSA decision layer. It fixes the state boundaries, analytical benchmarks, units, common universes, denominators, rules, costs, numerical conventions, sensitivity families and release conditions before the new diagnostics are calculated. The machine-readable specification is R13_EMPIRICAL_PROTOCOL_EN_GB.json. The companion source register, query manifest, disclosure design and input/output contract are identified at the end of this document.

The research question is whether alternative operational boundaries identify the same units under a declared descriptive follow-up rule, and how their plug-in semantic mismatch and action-set disagreement depend on an explicit benchmark. The benchmarks are constructed from the same state registers as the candidate indicators. They are not independently observed productivity, welfare, policy success or research-quality outcomes. Consequently, this extension can illustrate consequences of a measurement choice but cannot establish external construct validity or causal policy loss.

R11 remains the canonical completed article and computational baseline. Its manuscript, Online Resource 1, frozen S2 archive, figures and historical results remain unchanged. R12 remains the completed counter-audit and four-phase plan. This amendment is prospective for the newly specified diagnostics. It is not a retrospective preregistration of the original FAT analysis, a public registry deposit or a new author signature. The accepted package is dated and hash-bound before Phase 2.

The work executed during this empirical subtask was limited to reading public historical code and protocols, checking primary documentation, validating the declared specification and checking the existence, size and SHA-256 of the retained FAT input. No private CSV row was parsed, no new FAT metric or G* was calculated, no EU27 cohort was acquired and no new Monte Carlo or empirical replay was run. No login, account, API key, payment, terms acceptance or external deposit was performed.

2. Information known before the amendment

3. Common analytical contract

3.1 Units, distributions and weighting

Let G* denote the common eligible analytical universe. It is fixed once for an application and is shared by every boundary, benchmark and comparison within that application. Let p_g(s) be the complete-case state proportion for state s in unit g. Every p_g is nonnegative and sums exactly to one. For boundary D_d and benchmark A_q, the scores are a_gd = sum over s in D_d of p_g(s), and q_g = sum over s in A_q of p_g(s).

All cross-unit semantic averages and action losses give every eligible stratum/country weight one. FAT survey weights operate within a source stratum; they are not used to pool strata into a global adoption rate. OA publication counts operate within a country; larger publication-producing countries do not receive a larger weight in the comparison of country selections. The two India source strata remain distinct. International OA publications can contribute to several country counts, so country scores are not asserted to be independent.

3.2 Primary and secondary rules

The primary rule selects the three units with the lowest score. This represents a fixed, small descriptive review capacity: which low-prevalence units would be flagged for follow-up under each boundary? It is an illustration, not an actual allocation of resources or an empirically optimised policy. Three is inherited from the synthetic design; it is not justified by a claimed external policy standard.

The first secondary rule selects the three highest-score units. It describes identifying high-prevalence examples and checks whether conclusions depend on direction. It is not a substitute primary analysis. The second secondary rule selects every unit whose score is strictly below 1/4. Equality at 1/4 is excluded. The threshold is one-quarter as an illustrative reference inherited from the earlier grid, not a validated adoption target or OA requirement. No other k, threshold or direction will be searched in this extension.

The same rule, parameter, score denominator and priority apply to each candidate definition and the benchmark. A comparison of selections constructed on different eligible universes is not accepted.

3.3 Ties and numerical comparisons

Selection uses exact rational scores. Exact equality is resolved using the fixed unit priority specified below, shared by all definitions and the benchmark. Neither rounded display values nor a tolerance may merge scores for selection. For a rank rule, an exact tie between the kth and (k+1)th scores is flagged. A separate flag records whether that boundary gap is at most 1e-12; it is diagnostic only and never changes membership.

For each score vector separately, a secondary all-admissible membership envelope describes which units are guaranteed or possible if exact point ties may be resolved in any admissible way. It does not combine an independently favourable tie resolution for one definition with another favourable resolution for the benchmark to claim an exact loss bound. The primary action loss remains a single, reproducible value under the common fixed priority.

3.4 Semantic and action costs

Both cost systems use the numerical profiles equal_costs=(1,1), exclusion_costly=(1,2) and inclusion_costly=(2,1), in the order false inclusion, false exclusion. They are explicitly separate cost systems. A unit of semantic cost concerns probability mass assigned to different state sets. A unit of action cost concerns selection of a source stratum/country. The coefficients are assumed relative costs, not estimated monetary values or actual losses. [LOCAL02]

For semantic mismatch, equal costs are primary. Both asymmetric profiles are secondary for all three boundaries. Define FP_sem(g,d) as the mass in D_d but outside A_q, and FN_sem(g,d) as the mass in A_q but outside D_d. The reported semantic loss is:

L_S(d;c) = [sum over g in G* of {c_FP FP_sem(g,d) + c_FN FN_sem(g,d)}] / |G*|.

Report the two mean masses as well as their cost-weighted sum. These are plug-in differences between declared state sets, not observed error rates against an independently validated outcome. If D_d is a subset of A_q, false-inclusion mass is zero by design. If A_q is a subset of D_d, false-exclusion mass is zero by design. Such zeros are algebraic consequences, not new empirical discoveries or validation findings.

For action loss, let B_d,r and B_q,r be the candidate and benchmark selections. False selection is |B_d,r minus B_q,r| and missed reference is |B_q,r minus B_d,r|. Report the count-scale loss c_FP times false selection plus c_FN times missed reference, and divide that value by |G*| for the per-unit loss. The denominator is not k, the selected count, the union size, the number of establishments or the number of publications. With asymmetric costs, per-unit loss can exceed one; it is an average assumed cost, not a probability.

Equal action costs are used for the primary lowest-three rule and both secondary rules. The two asymmetric action profiles are evaluated only for the threshold rule. Two rank sets of the same size have equal false-selection and missed-reference counts. Thus the 1:2 and 2:1 profiles coincide for a fixed k and cannot demonstrate a directional cost trade-off there.

3.5 Set statistics and empty cases

For each of the three definitions versus q, report objective-relative action loss and set comparisons. For each of the three definition pairs, report set comparisons without relabelling the second definition as a separately justified objective. Set comparisons include equality, intersection and union sizes, symmetric difference and its division by |G*|, Jaccard and rank churn where applicable.

Jaccard is intersection/union for a nonempty union. When both sets are empty, retain the R11 convention Jaccard=1 and attach the status both_empty_convention_one, together with both cardinalities. Equality and symmetric difference zero remain true, but this convention is not positive evidence of substantive robustness. Rank churn is symmetric-difference size divided by 2k, only for the registered rank comparisons with equal positive k=3. It is null/not_applicable for threshold comparisons even if their realised sizes happen to be equal. Empty G* is a hard failure.

The internal canonical calculation uses exact integers or rational representations. Decimal displays are derived after calculation. Public outputs contain only authorised estimates and their authorised denominators, including |G*|. Protected FAT source-level denominators and any components exposing them remain internal. No p-values, confidence intervals, bootstrap, sampling-variance estimate or causal interpretation is added.

The unit is the exact country source label, not a pooled country total. The fixed priority is:

Bangladesh; Brazil; BurkinaFaso; Cambodia; Chile; Croatia; Ethiopia; Georgia; Ghana; India; India_Wave2_New; Kenya; Korea; Poland; Senegal; Vietnam.

4.3 Boundaries and benchmark

The fixed definition order is:

1. integrated_planning = {erp}.
2. specialised_planning = {specialised_software, erp}.
3. digitally_enabled_planning = {standard_software, mobile_apps_or_digital_platforms, specialised_software, erp}.

The primary benchmark is platform_or_more_specific_planning = {mobile_apps_or_digital_platforms, specialised_software, erp}. It corresponds to the existing synthetic dedicated_or_integrated_objective state set. The substantive question is routine planning through an application/platform, specialised software or ERP, beyond generic standard software alone. The shorthand platform-or-higher identifies this set; it does not demonstrate a universal capability hierarchy or higher productivity.

This objective lies between the specialised and broad digital sets but matches neither. Its being unmatched is a mathematical property, not the reason to choose it. Because it is built from the same observations, a lower loss against it means closer agreement with this analytical scope, not independent validity. Matching-boundary zero-loss cases are synthetic implementation controls and will not be presented as empirical validation.

4.4 Weights, arithmetic and reconciliation

The primary denominator D_g is the sum of positive finite base_wt over mapped rows in source stratum g. The same denominator is used for every d and q. First preserve the historical missing-token and JavaScript Number validity classification. For retained positive finite weights, convert the original decimal/scientific token exactly into a rational and sum exactly. The accepted grammar is fixed in the JSON. A finite positive token accepted by the historical parser but outside this grammar causes a halt; it is not silently reinterpreted.

This is new exact arithmetic on the recorded decimal weights. It is not claimed to reproduce sequential floating-point summation bit for bit. Before new action or semantic metrics, the adapter must reconcile the registered taxonomy/counts, weight eligibility, suppression flags and released historical outputs. Integer counts and flags must agree exactly. Comparable rate/summary values must agree within absolute 1e-12. The unchanged historical source, or its transparently observed in-memory execution, supplies the comparison; full source-stratum results must not be written into a public report. The reconciliation report contains only check counts, status and maximum residuals. Any failure stops the extension before new metrics. The historical code and outputs remain byte-unchanged. [LOCAL07, LOCAL10]

The tolerance is for numerical reconciliation only. Exact rational selection ties are a separate convention and are not replaced with a 1e-12 tie band.

4.5 Fixed common eligibility G*_FAT

A source enters G*_FAT only if it belongs to the fixed roster, has at least 100 mapped rows and at least 100 positive-weight mapped contributors, and has a positive denominator D_g. For each of the five state atoms, its all-mapped count and its positive-weight contributor count must each be either zero or at least five. All three definitions and q must also satisfy the historical binary positive/negative small-cell screen. The state-atom screen covers every contrast atom generated by these four membership patterns. Input, taxonomy and reconciliation gates must have passed.

This is an explicit new common-universe screen, stronger than an endpoint-only screen. It protects the population that actually contributes weighted mass and prevents exchanging original 14/16/16 denominators across comparisons. It does not certify privacy. The historical minimum of 30 for positive-class composition is not imposed: no new within-positive-class composition tables are proposed.

At least ten strata must remain. This retains the project minimum for cross-stratum summaries and avoids a three-unit rule dominating a very small surviving roster. It is not a statistical-power threshold, a producer requirement or a confidentiality guarantee. If G* has fewer than ten strata, the new numerical FAT extension is reported as not estimable under the specified release design. Neither the benchmark nor the screens may be relaxed to obtain a favourable result. G* has not been calculated during R13.

The identical G* is used for all boundaries, q, rules, costs and the weighting sensitivity. No pairwise deletion or rule-specific universe is allowed.

4.6 Weighting and unresolved rows

The single weighting sensitivity removes weight magnitudes from exactly the same positive-weight mapped contributors as the primary analysis. Each state mass becomes its count in that matched row set divided by the total number of those contributors. Recalculate only primary lowest-three comparisons and equal-cost semantic mismatch. This isolates the effect of weight magnitudes. It is not the historical all-mapped unweighted sensitivity, which has a different row universe and remains unchanged.

No new FAT uncertainty interval or unresolved-state action-loss analysis is specified. The historical source calculates the mass of the positive-weight unresolved subset, so that statistic is not denied. However, the admissibility of all unresolved observations to the five states and a full weight mass for rows with missing/invalid weights are not established. The new normalised FAT input therefore has unresolved_total=null and unresolved_status=not_identifiable; null is not zero. The explanatory reason remains in separate protocol/adapter evidence. Original conditional unresolved bounds remain in R11 with their original assumptions. [LOCAL06, LOCAL09]

5.4 Response gates, denominators and common universe

A valid response has HTTP 200 and bounded JSON. Returned group keys must be unique and limited to the six registered states or literal unknown. Counts and meta.count must be nonnegative integers, with Boolean values rejected. The sum of all group counts must equal meta.count. Nested grouping and nonempty unrequested work-record payloads are not accepted; an absent or empty results list is harmless. The fixed domain has at most seven buckets and per_page is 100.

When groups_count is returned, it must equal the number of returned buckets. When next_cursor is returned, it must be null. Neither optional field is required to exist. The fixed enum, count equality and capacity checks remain required; groups_count alone would not establish complete pagination. A missing recognised bucket may be zero-filled only after those checks pass. Unknown absence then means zero unknown records; no mismatch residual is imputed as unknown or closed. [WEB06]

For each country, N=meta.count, M is the count of literal unknown and D=N-M is the sum of the six known-status counts. Primary scores divide each positive numerator by D. All d and q use that same known-status denominator. Publish N, M, D and M/N as separate coverage information. M/N concerns missing OA status among matched works, not all missing affiliation or indexing coverage.

All 27 predeclared countries must have valid complete responses with N>0 and D>0. If so, G*_OA is exactly the full roster. If any fails, report the acquisition/denominator failure instead of a reduced decision analysis. A valid country's zero OA rate or unfavourable result never removes it.

5.5 Unknown-status sensitivity and shared publications

The OA sensitivity is allowed only when M is identified from a validated response. For each d and q separately, it uses the interval [P/(D+M), (P+M)/(D+M)], where P is the corresponding known-state count. M=0 gives a degenerate interval. Missing M is not zero and blocks this sensitivity.

The assumption is that an unknown-status matched work could occupy any of the six registered states. It is a sensitivity model, not a verification of upstream classification. Report marginal guaranteed/possible/excluded membership for lowest-three under all-admissible ties, and guaranteed/ambiguous/excluded membership for the strict below-one-quarter rule.

These are rectangular marginal outer-envelope certificates. The same international work can appear in several countries. Its unknown OA status must be the same in each appearance, so assignments couple country counts. Grouped country totals do not identify the overlap structure or the full joint feasible set. Within a country, the same unknown work also couples all definitions and q. Rectangularity is therefore not asserted for the actual common cohort.

Guaranteed or excluded membership established over the outer box remains a sufficient certificate for a feasible subset. A unit that is merely possible in the outer box is not claimed to be attainable in the actual joint cohort. No independent best/worst assignment across d and q, exact joint action-loss bound, optimised scenario search or statistical confidence interval is reported.

6. Fixed analysis families and reporting

P1, primary: complete-case lowest-three. For each d versus q, calculate equal-cost action loss and set statistics. For the three d-versus-d pairs, calculate set statistics. FAT uses exact weighted masses; OA uses known-status counts.

P2, primary: complete-case semantic mismatch. For each d, retain mean false-inclusion mass, mean false-exclusion mass and equal-cost L_S.

S1, secondary: the P1 comparison structure with highest-three and equal costs.

S2, secondary: strict below-one-quarter with equal costs and both asymmetric action-cost profiles against q. Pairwise set statistics are calculated once. Empty and all-selected cases are retained.

S3, secondary: both asymmetric semantic-cost profiles on the same complete-case masses and G*. No interpretation transfers between semantic and action units.

S4, FAT only: matched-row unweighted P1 and P2 on the same positive-weight contributors and G*.

S5, secondary: exact boundary-tie flags, near-gap flags and separate all-admissible point-membership envelopes for lowest-three and highest-three, one vector at a time. FAT source identities are not published.

S6, OA only: marginal unknown-status membership certificates for lowest-three and below-one-quarter. These are envelopes, not coupled loss extrema.

No cross-product of additional objectives, thresholds, k values, weighting rules or cost profiles is explored. No CIS application, FAT regression, new simulation grid, licence-aware OA extension or new FAT missing-state interval is added. All registered eligible comparisons and zero/no-change results are retained. Scientific outcomes do not select which family is reported. A failed data gate or disclosure review may withhold a complete predefined block with an explicit reason; withheld does not mean zero.

7. Cumulative FAT disclosure conditions

The project continues its hardened public-release restrictions: no establishment rows, source-level state/rate vectors, source denominators, exact source weight totals, source exclusions, rankings, selected IDs or eligibility membership lists are published. The state-atom screen is necessary for the proposed computation but is not itself publication approval. These are project safeguards, not a new producer authorisation. [LOCAL04–LOCAL11]

Before release, construct a cumulative ledger of the full already distributed corpus: R11 main/S1; both historical public FAT summaries; the empirical replay and R10 post-hoc reports in ESM_2 and frozen S2; and R12/R13 text repeating those values. Inventory exact/rounded totals, extrema, quantiles, contrasts, eligibility counts, inequalities and algebraic equivalents. Hash files and de-duplicate repeated evidence values. Include the threat of auxiliary knowledge about the public source roster and source totals.

The candidate blocks are FAT_PRIMARY (P1/P2), FAT_SECONDARY_RULES_COSTS (S1–S3), FAT_MATCHED_ROW_WEIGHTING (S4) and FAT_TIES (S5). They contain only cross-unit scalar summaries and a cleared common eligible count, without identities or source-level vectors. Trace every proposed field to its underlying state atoms, units and denominator. Review the proposed blocks jointly with the old release for complementary recovery, differencing and combinations that reconstruct suppressed cells or prohibited source-specific facts. A cost transformation or duplicate ratio is not independent evidence and must not bypass suppression.

Where a concrete reconstruction attack can be encoded, retain its constraints and test the feasible values of implicated suppressed cells. A recovered 1–4 cell, singleton recovery of a prohibited source statistic or unavoidable source identification fails the relevant release. An unimplemented or intractable test is not called a pass. A second reviewer records the bounded inventory, attack checks and uncovered auxiliary information. There is no promise of universal anonymity, differential privacy or a complete privacy proof.

If reconstruction risk remains unresolved, or the inventory is incomplete, withhold the whole affected block and record a generic reason without leaking the protected values. Re-review across blocks: suppressing a field while releasing an algebraic equivalent is insufficient. If primary blocks cannot be cleared, retain the method, toy demonstration and original FAT study rather than inventing a publishable extension. These fail-closed rules are fixed now; the disclosure outcome has not been obtained in R13.
